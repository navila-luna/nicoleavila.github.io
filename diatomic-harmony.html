1 · Overview

Learning harmony through interaction

Diatomic Harmony connects music learning with interface design. I conceived, designed, and built it in response to a problem in my own music practice.

As I developed as a singer, songwriter, and producer, I found it difficult to create chord progressions that carried the emotion of a story while also working with my voice and delivery. Stopping to remember which notes or chords belonged together interrupted the creative process.

I translated the structure of diatonic harmony into an algorithm and an interactive experience. The app lets me explore and loop chord progressions, see the chords clearly, and then take a pattern I like back to the piano. Building the underlying logic also became part of how I learned the music theory.

The result is useful to me first: an interactive way to learn without feeling lost or needing years of piano and music-theory training before experimenting. I hope it can also support other emerging singers, songwriters, and musicians who lack access to lessons or experienced collaborators.

2 · Motivation and problem

Music education is not equally accessible

When I looked for private piano instruction in my area, hour-long sessions commonly cost an estimated $50–$100. A 2026 multi-city pricing guide places the national average for a 60-minute piano lesson at about $87. That cost can put individualized music education out of reach for low-income families and create a high barrier for beginners who are interested but unsure whether lessons are worth the investment.

I can afford lessons now, but they are still expensive—especially alongside other activities that matter to my creative practice and well-being, including singing, improv, and fitness classes. I created Diatomic Harmony to explore how technology could offer a more accessible starting point for learning music theory, chord progressions, and songwriting independently.

3 · Research or design question

The focus: diatonic harmony

How might an interactive tool make diatonic harmony—and the relationships between chords within a key—easier to explore for an independent learner developing a songwriting practice?

4 · Contributions and implementation

Product, music, and development

  • Product direction: Defined the experience around preserving creative flow and supporting independent exploration rather than testing memorization.
  • Music system: Translated diatonic relationships into an algorithm for generating and exploring chord progressions.
  • Application development: Built the interface in React, used Tone.js for chord playback and looping, and used Zustand for state management.
  • Development process: Iterated around my own songwriting workflow—exploring progressions in the app, listening to them loop, and then transferring promising patterns to the piano.
  • AI-assisted development: Used ChatGPT as a development aid, including prompt engineering, while making the product, musical, and implementation decisions.

5 · Challenges and reflections

Learning the theory well enough to model it

The hardest part was teaching myself diatonic harmony through free online resources, then understanding it well enough to translate it into an algorithm. I also had to decide which chords to prioritize so the app could support meaningful exploration without overwhelming a beginner.
